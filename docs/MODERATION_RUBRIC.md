# Story Moderation Rubric

## Publication standard

A story should be publishable only when it is useful, first-person, privacy-aware and proportionate to what the contributor directly experienced.

## Review dimensions

| Dimension | Publishable standard |
| --- | --- |
| First-person basis | The contributor describes what they experienced, observed or were told. |
| Privacy | Names, direct identifiers, confidential records and vulnerable-person details are removed. |
| Accuracy of framing | Opinion, interpretation and fact are not presented as the same thing. |
| Proportionality | One role, team or period is not turned into a universal claim about the employer. |
| Usefulness | The story explains what changed and gives a future candidate something practical to ask. |
| Nuance | Positive or workable parts are retained when they are relevant to the contributor’s account. |
| Safety | No threats, doxxing, discriminatory abuse or material the contributor has no right to share. |
| AI boundaries | AI has not invented events, intensified accusations or replaced contributor approval. |

## Moderator actions

- **Publish** — all standards are satisfied.
- **Request changes** — the story can become publishable after specific revisions.
- **Reject** — the content is unsafe, unusable or outside the platform purpose.
- **Unpublish** — a published story later creates a privacy, legal or safety concern.
- **Withdraw** — the contributor requests removal.

## Required public framing

Use only labels supported by the current product state:

- Anonymous contributor
- Contributor approved
- Human moderated
- Contributor perspective

Do not use employee-verification labels until a real verification workflow exists.

## Candidate value check

Before publication, the moderator should be able to answer:

> What better question can a candidate ask after reading this story?

If the answer is unclear, request more context or decline publication.
